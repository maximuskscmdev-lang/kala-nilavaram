# Content Moderation Policy — Kala Nilavaram

**Status: FIRST DRAFT — requires review by a lawyer or child-safety/
education-law consultant in Tamil Nadu before public launch**, given minors
are reporting on adults/institutions through this platform.

_Last updated: [DATE]_

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how content, reviews, and whistleblower reports are
reviewed on Kala Nilavaram, and the roles/safeguards involved, so
contributors and readers can trust the process without us exposing
individual cases.

## 2. Roles

- **Editor** — school-level or platform-wide; approves/rejects news, study
  content, and event submissions. Always a student or platform staff role,
  **never a teacher**, to avoid conflicts of interest.
- **Moderator** — reviews the whistleblower inbox; elevated, audit-logged
  access. Always a student or platform staff role, **never a current
  teacher** at any school they moderate.
- **School Admin** — platform-side tenant manager; oversees editors/
  moderators for their school, sees tenant analytics.
- **Super Admin** — platform founders; cross-tenant oversight and final
  escalation point.

Teachers may publish content, submit whistleblower reports, and leave
school-level reviews — but hold **no moderation or editorial authority**
over student-submitted whistleblower reports or reviews, by design.

## 3. Editorial standards (news, study content, events)

- **News/events**: submission → in-review → published/rejected, with
  editor comments returned to the author. Editors check for: factual
  accuracy to the extent verifiable, defamatory or identifying content
  about private individuals, and adherence to category/tagging norms.
  Anonymity or a pen name never bypasses this review.
- **Study content**: lighter-touch — auto-published, then post-hoc
  moderated (spot-checked and responsive to reports), since the risk
  profile is lower than news or complaints.
- **Aggregated news**: never republishes full third-party article text —
  headline, short summary, publish date, source, and outbound link only.

## 4. Review-authenticity standards

- Only verified students/teachers can submit reviews; review counts
  toward public ratings only after moderator sign-off.
- Rate limit: one review per school per account per 30 days (enforced at
  the application layer).
- Anti-gaming detection: moderators watch for review-brigading patterns —
  e.g., a burst of reviews mentioning the same teacher name in a short
  window — and can flag/hold a batch for manual review before it affects
  public ratings or Best Teacher recognition scoring.
- Teacher reviewers are restricted (at the database level, not just
  policy) from targeting a named colleague — teacher reviews only cover
  administration, management, facilities, and working environment.

## 5. Whistleblower / complaint escalation criteria

Every report moves through: **Received → Under Review → Verified/Contacted
→ Action Taken / Escalated / Closed**, logged at each step.

- **Triage**: a moderator verifies the report by contacting the submitter
  personally, using the encrypted contact details captured at submission.
- **Severity assessment** determines the path: internal follow-up with
  school contacts, escalation to a parent/guardian or external authority
  for serious safety issues, or closing with a generic public-facing
  reason.
- **Immediate-risk safety flag**: if a report involves abuse, self-harm
  risk, or violence, a moderator marks it as an explicit safety flag. This
  visibly prompts every moderator viewing the report toward real-world
  escalation (school counselor, parent/guardian, local authority, or a
  helpline) — the system is designed so a safety-critical report can never
  simply sit unresolved in a queue. This platform has no legal
  mandatory-reporting obligation identified at the time of writing, but
  this safeguard exists regardless, and **this specific workflow should be
  reviewed by a lawyer or child-safety consultant in Tamil Nadu before
  launch.**
- **Never public by default**: individual complaint pages are never
  indexed by search engines, never exposed via any public API, and never
  shown to anyone outside the assigned moderation team. Aggregated,
  de-identified summaries (e.g., "issues raised this month") may be
  published by the editorial team, but only after removing names and
  identifying detail.

## 6. Public-facing summary of how complaints are handled

We publish this section publicly (e.g., linked from the whistleblower
submission page) so submitters know what to expect without us exposing any
individual case:

> Every report gets a private tracking ID so you can check status without
> logging back in. A small, trusted moderator team reviews and verifies
> each report by contacting you directly, then decides on a path — internal
> follow-up, escalation to a parent/guardian or outside authority for
> serious safety concerns, or closing with an explanation. Reports
> flagged as involving immediate risk are escalated in the real world, not
> just tracked in-app. Your name and contact details are never shown
> publicly or to your school.

## 7. Escalation ladder (internal)

1. School-level Moderator triages and verifies.
2. For financial/administrative mismanagement or facilities issues:
   internal follow-up, typically contacting a school liaison the platform
   has vetted, or advising the submitter on other channels if none exists.
3. For harassment, discrimination, or safety concerns: default posture is
   escalation to parent/guardian and, where appropriate, an external
   authority — Super Admin sign-off recommended before contacting any
   external authority on a minor's behalf.
4. Super Admin is the final internal escalation point for disputes about
   how a report was handled.

## 8. Appeals

Authors of rejected content, and subjects of published reviews/articles,
may appeal a moderation decision once, to a different reviewer than the
original one, per the Terms of Service takedown/appeals process.

## 9. Transparency commitments

- Best Teacher recognition scoring formula is published once, publicly,
  and the score breakdown for each round's winners is published alongside
  the announcement.
- This Content Moderation Policy itself is public and versioned; material
  changes will be dated and summarized at the top of this document.
